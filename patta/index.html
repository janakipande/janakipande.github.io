Chennai, Injambakkam

March 2025

The patta mystery behind Chennai’s rapid development

As Chennai expands, builders like Baashyaam and G-Square can hardly be ignored. Their numerous ads colonise the surfaces of buses, autorickshaws, electricity poles, and newspapers front pages, questioning the legality of this boom. Development is inevitable, but in the neighbourhood of Injambakkam, where the affluent and the underprivileged coexist, only the latter face challenges of relocation, police threats, and electricity being cut off. Less than a hundred metres away, new plots of G-Square are being developed into luxury villas.

Development in Injambakkam has been debated about lately. Three bridges were proposed to be reconstructed over the Buckingham Canal to link the OMR and ECR. The new bridges would have an increased width to ease the traffic congestion during the peak office hours.

The East Coast Road (ECR) has numerous tributaries that lead to the villages scattered along the route. Two such narrow roads lead to contrasting realities of Injambakkam. One is marked with grand villas, marble floors, large glass windows and balconies, stone carvings and fountains. Security cameras peek over the silent streets where Bentleys, Mini Coopers, and BMWs remain parked. The other leads to crammed narrow aisles, paths muddy after morning showers, walls with chipped paint, broken gates, cows munching on garbage, and the stench of open drains with flies hovering over them.

The government claims a large part of Injambakkam belongs to them, and wants to get rid of the encroachers who do not have a patta. However, on the other side, a resident, Vedagiri, supposedly connected to a prominent DMK minister, claimed that they too bought the land from a zamindar whose name he cannot recall.

The ownership of the land in Injambakkam is unclear as some say the land is theirs, but the government claims to own it. Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam(DMK) spokesperson TKS Elangovan recalled, “Land in that area used to be farmland, but now since farming stopped, we bought the land from them.”

What is the lingering problem?

“They have been planning to relocate these people who are residing here for the last six years, but it has not begun yet,” said Akash Maheshwari, 37, who owns an eatery in the same neighbourhood. He claimed that the land on Thiruvalluvar Salai, from the St. John’s school till the Hanuman Colony, is a non patta land, that is it belongs to the government. “The land was sold to the people many years down the line. They refuse to accept the compensation given by that of the Government and probably that is why the relocation is still delayed. There is an ongoing litigation too” he added.

Maheshwari talks about how the government was planning to build a hospital and a bus stand there. The residents have protested multiple times. He explains that as much as the government wants to develop the land, they do not want the massive vote count from the area to crumble. Greater Chennai Police has rampaged the area several times asking the residents to vacate their houses. The government has cut off the electricity supply from the houses and shops. The residents somehow continue to thrive by adopting various methods to sustain their livelihoods. “People from the Government or the Corporation never show up,” he said.

An MTC bus time keeper, Haridas, who lives in the poorer side of Injambakam with his wife Shanti said, “We will not accept the government’s plan.” They have filed a case in the Supreme Court and say that they will not move out. However, they admit that they do not have the patta of that land, but Haridas added, “This was also farming land. The villagers sold it to us.” Haridas explains that the dispute is not restricted to a few households, but 260 acres, which the government is claiming as its own. “The court will decide now,” he said.

However, the 260 acres that the government claims as its own begins right at the boundary of the affluent neighbourhood, and Haridas said, “They say this side is government land, but what about that side? In that whole area G-Square is building houses.” A neighbour, refusing to be identified, alleged G-Square to have high ruling party connections. “They will not have any patta problems, but they also got the land from the same people who sold it to us,” he said.

Jaybal, is a caretaker of one of the houses in the affluent neighbourhood. Where Koi fishes swim in the silence, he admitted in soft whispers that his neighbour is a relative of a prominent DMK minister. “My boss wanted to buy that house first, but when we made a bid for it, the very next day it was already bought.” He explained that while it took them nearly half a decade to rebuild the neighbouring house and move in, the process was significantly faster for their neighbours. “They are a big family, every member has a car.” He explains that he has been there only for a year, but he added, “The area is full of people like this, people with strong connections.”

What is the land acquisition process and the DMK’s take?

M. Kanchana, tahsildar at the Chennai Collectorate, sitting behind stacks of dust shrouded files, explained that when it comes to acquiring lands or infrastructure projects related to the government, a requisition letter is submitted to the Tehsildar’s office, which is then followed by a tehsildar’s report. Only after a series of field inspections and reports where the involved parties take proper measurements, a survey is done to estimate the amount of land required, and the costs involved. It is then followed by submitting another report for approval of funds to compensate people and begin the required work.

Kanchana explains that there are four main acts governing this procedure, the Tamil Nadu Land Acquisition Act 1894, the Tamil Nadu Industrial Purpose Act 1997, the Tamil Nadu Highways Act 2001, and the Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition Rehabilitation, Resettlement Act 2013(LARR Act, 2013), which has been followed since 2018.

“When we take people’s land we always give them land and compensation money for relocation or reconstruction,” said DMK spokesperson, TKS Elangovan. They purchase that land at market price. “But in Injambakkam, people are being cheated by others, and for that the government cannot start paying for its own land. That whole area was not ours. The parts that they say are private land, could have been farm lands bought by the zamindars,” he added.

“The government cannot do anything in Injambakkam, there are high court rulings,” said Elangovan. He elaborated, “Big builders normally don’t encroach on government land because if they build high rise buildings on government land, it will be a huge loss and they will have to face litigation from their buyers too.”

What roles do builders like Baashyaam and G-Square play?

Baashyaam and G-Square are two leading luxury construction groups in Tamil Nadu which have alleged political connections with the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam(DMK). From the MTC buses, to hundreds of billboards that dot the Chennai cityscape, advertisements scream these two names loud and clear for the population to know about them. However what is not so loud and clear is their land acquisition procedures. There is no information regarding their land purchasing procedures or the criteria in doing the same on their official websites. In both of the registered offices, officials said that there is only one individual who looks after the land acquisition procedures and refused to share their contact numbers. Neither of them were reachable over emails as well.

The Organiser, in 2023 published an article highlighting that G-Square, founded in 2012, had reported losses of seven crore rupees in 2020, and eight crore rupees in 2021. However, their turnover rose to Rs 2000 crores in the second half of 2021, as the DMK came into power. As per the Real Estate Regulatory Authority(RERA) records, from 2017 to 2020, G Square had no business.

What do the architects have to say?

Sreenivasan Vinod, an independent architect drew a sharp contrast to what Maheshwari said. Situated at a five minute walk from Vedagiri’s residence, Vinod was inspecting a property at a Radiance Group construction site. He said that the area is rapidly developing and seeing a rise in new projects. “But this land actually belongs to the people, and the government is taking it up for the construction of the ECR-OMR link road,” he claimed. He added that the original residents of the place are being forcefully relocated along the ECR-OMR stretch towards Thiruporur.

Vinod explained that properties around Injambakkam are mainly being purchased by the G-Square group. “The G-Square group has strong political holds, this is a posh area, and of course there are lots of influential people living here. I am on various projects, and some of them belong to prominent ones, but I cannot disclose their identities” Vinod added.

Senthil, an architect who works for Chennai Metropolitan Development Authority(CMDA) said that the Government strictly adheres to the LARR Act. There are no lapses other than when there is some dispute and the court comes into play. “The relocated people as a result of the ECR-OMR link road construction will be given proper compensations. What else do you expect? When someone buys your house, they will just pay you the money right? Why should they pay for your housing? The government does not owe them anything. That is how it works,” he said.

What does the BJP think of this?

A Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) member Ranganayakulu Ponnuru talked about the presence of internal loopholes in the current state government of Tamil Nadu. “Most of the people have no idea about what is happening in the city. If the government is transparent, it automatically solves a major part of the problem,” he said.

He said that the government portrays that the upcoming constructions in the city is contributing to the glittery image of ‘Smart City’ but at what costs. There are neither proper measures taken for the relocated people as a part of these booming projects. “Relocated people are often mistreated because they come from various corners of the country. They are asked to pay money to the ward members, settle in a place and after a few months they are asked to evict. The government takes patta on the land where they lived with the same money that they had previously collected. It is a huge scam that has been going on,” he said.

Ponnuru adds that the fuel to the fire is the lack of awareness and knowledge about this deep rooted corruption. People living in their houses for generations may have no clue that it does not really belong to them, but the government. Ponnuru further stresses how this is going on in the whole state.

“What the DMK government does is that it decides where to build a road or a bridge and then goes on to purchase all the land around the proposed area. It then sells it at a higher price because of the newly constructed road or bridge,” he explained. “Annamalai also released DMK files alleging corruption and named G Square,” claimed Ponnuru.

People should come forward and register complaints, he stated, “However, who would want to risk their lives. One road accident and the entire case against the government will be closed. Tamil Nadu is not a very good state.”

What is the Greater Chennai Corporation’s opinion?

The Greater Chennai Corporation(GCC) has denied all the relocation claims. The Superintending Engineer of the Bridges Department in GCC, B Shankaravelu said, “There is absolutely no relocation. The bridges are just getting replaced.” He added that there will be no disturbance to the residents.

Contrastingly, the bridges were proposed to be reconstructed to increase their width to ease the traffic congestion during the peak office hours. Residents of the Pandian Salai area along the Canal Puram Road complained that they had been asked multiple times by Baashyaam to vacate their residences. The Bridges department has denied all these claims.

The conflict does not end anytime soon, does it?

The conflict surrounding land ownership and relocation in places like Injambakkam sheds light on power dynamics, political influence, and the plight of economically disadvantaged communities. For over six years, residents have faced uncertainty and threats, while luxury developments by groups like Baashyaam and G-Square flourish nearby as a result of their strong influential holds.

The lack of transparency in land acquisition processes, coupled with allegations of corruption and political connections remains largely unnoticed by the larger masses. The issue is further exacerbated by the lack of proof of any illegal activity. The silence surrounding these struggles reveals the urgent need for awareness and accountability in addressing the injustice faced by those most vulnerable.
